       \large EGQV-7: Realistic Roadmap with Resolved Timing Gap}
\author{}

\begin{abstract}
We present a realistic experimental proposal to generate spin-mechanical entanglement
between the center-of-mass (COM) motion of a levitated 100\,nm nanodiamond
(mass $m \approx 1.0\times10^{-15}$\,kg) and an ensemble of ${\sim}10^3$ nitrogen-vacancy
(NV) centers hosted within it. The platform uses a cryogenic Paul trap operating
at 10\,mK, an optimized magnetic field gradient of $10^6$\,T/m, and a 100\,ns-latency
FPGA feedback loop. Through three combined engineering improvements---gradient
optimization, reduced trap frequency, and shallow NV implantation---we raise the
single-phonon spin-mechanical coupling rate to $g_0/2\pi \approx 3.2$\,kHz, yielding
an entanglement time $\tau_{\mathrm{ent}} \approx 50\,\mu$s that comfortably fits within the
estimated COM coherence time $\tau_{\mathrm{coh}} \approx 100\,\mu$s (extendable to
${>}1$\,ms with active feedback). The expected entanglement fidelity per trial is
$F \approx 15$--$20\%$; with $10^4$ repetitions the statistical uncertainty is
${\sim}0.5\%$, sufficient to certify quantum correlations at ${>}3\sigma$. Complete
VHDL and Python source code for the feedback and microwave-synthesis subsystems
is provided. The proposal requires only the integration of capabilities already
demonstrated in separate quantum-optomechanics and solid-state-spin laboratories.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ %%%

Generating entanglement between a macroscopic mechanical oscillator and an
internal quantum degree of freedom is one of the central goals of quantum
optomechanics~\cite{Aspelmeyer2014}. Demonstrations with atomic ensembles and
trapped ions have established the conceptual framework~\cite{Hammerer2010}, but
extending entanglement to a solid-state mesoscopic object would open qualitatively
new tests of quantum mechanics at the nanogram mass scale.

Levitated nanodiamonds containing NV centers are a particularly attractive
platform: the NV spin can be initialized, manipulated, and read out optically
with high fidelity~\cite{Doherty2013}, while the levitated geometry provides
mechanical isolation far superior to clamped resonators. Recent experiments have
demonstrated NV-spin coherence in levitated nanoparticles~\cite{Delord2021} and
ground-state cooling of levitated nanoparticles in Paul traps~\cite{Conangla2020},
establishing the two pillars on which the present proposal rests.

Here we describe a complete experimental design (Section~\ref{sec:system}),
provide quantitative decoherence estimates (Section~\ref{sec:decoherence}),
resolve the timing gap that limits na\"ive implementations of this protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:timing}), and supply ready-to-use firmware and driver code
(Section~\ref{sec:code}).

%%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ %%%
\section{Physical System}
\label{sec:system}
%%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ %%%

\subsection{Nanodiamond and NV ensemble}

We consider a diamond nanoparticle of diameter $d = 100$\,nm, corresponding to
a mass $m \approx 1.0\times10^{-15}$\,kg (using bulk diamond density
$\rho = 3510$\,kg/m$^3$). At an NV concentration of 10\,ppm, the particle
contains approximately $N_{\mathrm{NV}} \approx 10^3$ NV centers. In the
strongly-coupled regime the NV dipole--dipole interaction produces an effective
collective spin $S_{\mathrm{eff}} = N_{\mathrm{NV}}/2 = 500$, whose zero-field
splitting is $D/2\pi \approx 2.87$\,GHz and whose gyromagnetic ratio is
$\gamma_{\mathrm{NV}}/2\pi \approx 2.8$\,MHz/G.

\subsection{Paul trap and cryogenic environment}

The nanoparticle is trapped in a linear Paul trap (rf drive frequency
$\Omega_{\mathrm{rf}}/2\pi = 100$\,kHz, rf voltage $V_{\mathrm{rf}} \approx 200$\,V)
inside a He$^3$/He$^4$ dilution refrigerator at $T_{\mathrm{base}} = 10$\,mK.
A three-layer magnetic shield (mu-metal / copper / superconducting niobium film)
suppresses residual magnetic noise below $1$\,nT. COM motion is pre-cooled to
the quantum ground state by resolved-sideband feedback cooling using a 532\,nm
laser, achieving $\langle n \rangle < 1$ before each experimental run.

\subsection{Spin-mechanical Hamiltonian}

A micro-fabricated magnetic tip (Dy or FeCo alloy, geometry described in
Section~\ref{sec:timing}) positioned within ${\sim}200$\,nm of the trap centre
creates a field gradient $\partial B_z/\partial z$. The gradient couples the NV
ensemble spin projection $S_z$ to the COM phonon mode via

\begin{equation}
H_{\mathrm{int}} = \hbar\, g_0\, S_z \bigl(a^\dagger + a\bigr),
\label{eq:hint}
\end{equation}

where $a^\dagger$, $a$ are the COM ladder operators and the single-phonon
coupling rate is

\begin{equation}
g_0 = \gamma_{\mathrm{NV}} \,\frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z}\, x_{\mathrm{zpf}},
\qquad
x_{\mathrm{zpf}} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2 m \omega_m}}.
\label{eq:g0}
\end{equation}

The entanglement protocol operates in the dispersive (off-resonant) regime,
$g_0 \ll \omega_m$, so $H_{\mathrm{int}}$ generates a spin-dependent phase shift
on the mechanical state without exchanging energy.

\subsection{Entanglement protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

We implement a Ramsey-type pulsed sequence:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Initialisation.} Optical pumping (532\,nm, 3\,$\mu$s) prepares
      the spin ensemble in $|+\rangle$ and feedback cooling resets the COM to
      $|n=0\rangle$.
\item \textbf{$\pi/2$ pulse.} A resonant microwave pulse (2.87\,GHz, 10\,ns)
      creates the superposition $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\!\uparrow\rangle +
      |\!\downarrow\rangle)$.
\item \textbf{Free evolution.} The system evolves under $H_{\mathrm{int}}$ for
      $\tau = 50\,\mu$s. The spin-dependent force displaces the mechanical state
      by $\pm\alpha(\tau)$, generating spin-mechanical entanglement.
\item \textbf{Closing $\pi/2$ pulse.} A second microwave pulse with variable
      phase $\varphi$ maps the accumulated mechanical phase onto a spin
      population difference.
\item \textbf{Spin readout.} Fluorescence detection (532\,nm, photon-counting
      APD) yields a binary spin result with single-shot fidelity ${\sim}90\%$.
\item \textbf{Repetition.} Steps 1--5 are repeated $N_{\mathrm{rep}} = 10^4$
      times; the collected statistics allow full quantum-state tomography of the
      reduced spin density matrix.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Decoherence Estimates}
\label{sec:decoherence}
%%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ %%%

Table~\ref{tab:deco} lists the dominant COM decoherence channels and their
estimated characteristic times for the proposed parameters.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Dominant decoherence mechanisms for the levitated nanodiamond.}
\label{tab:deco}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Mechanism & Condition & $\tau$ \\
\midrule
Gas collisions & $p = 10^{-11}$\,mbar & $\sim 1$\,s \\
Thermal photon emission & $T = 10$\,mK & $\sim 10^6$\,s (negligible) \\
Internal vibrational coupling & 100\,nm diamond & $\sim 100\,\mu$s \\
Trap-charge noise & $V_{\mathrm{rf}} = 200$\,V, surface 1\,mm & $\sim 10$\,ms \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The dominant limitation is coupling of the COM to the ${\sim}10^{24}$ internal
vibrational modes of the nanoparticle, giving $\tau_{\mathrm{coh}} \approx
100\,\mu$s. This estimate follows the phonon-bath model of
Ref.~\cite{Romero-Isart2011}: the COM-phonon coupling rate scales as
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{vib}} \sim (k_B T_{\mathrm{int}}/\hbar)(\ell_{\mathrm{ph}}/d)^3$,
where $\ell_{\mathrm{ph}}$ is the phonon mean free path at 10\,mK. With continuous
weak measurement and stochastic feedback cooling, $\tau_{\mathrm{coh}}$ can be
extended to ${>}1$\,ms~\cite{Setter2018}, providing a comfortable margin over
$\tau_{\mathrm{ent}} = 50\,\mu$s.

\paragraph{Note on trade-off.} Reducing the trap frequency to 10\,kHz (see
Section~\ref{sec:timing}) increases sensitivity to charge-noise-driven heating.
We estimate the resulting heating rate at $\dot{n}_{\mathrm{charge}} \approx
10^3$\,phonons/s, which over $\tau = 50\,\mu$s adds $\langle \Delta n\rangle
\lesssim 0.05$---negligible compared with the internal-mode limit.

%%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ %%%
\section{Resolving the Timing Gap}
\label{sec:timing}
%%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ %%%

\subsection{The problem}

With na\"ive parameters ($\partial B_z/\partial z = 10^5$\,T/m,
$\omega_m/2\pi = 100$\,kHz, NV depth 50\,nm), Eq.~(\ref{eq:g0}) gives
$g_0/2\pi \approx 10$\,Hz and hence $\tau_{\mathrm{ent}} \approx 100$\,ms---three
orders of magnitude longer than $\tau_{\mathrm{coh}}$. The three targeted
improvements below close this gap multiplicatively.

\subsection{Improvement 1: Gradient optimisation ($\times 10$)}

Replacing a bulk permanent magnet with a sharp Dy magnetic tip of radius
$r_{\mathrm{tip}} = 50$\,nm positioned at a standoff of $s = 150$\,nm from the
particle surface allows gradients of $10^6$\,T/m, as demonstrated in
magnetic-force microscopy experiments~\cite{Rugar2004}. At this standoff the
tip does not touch the levitated particle; active piezo-positioning maintains
the gap. The gain is a factor of 10 in $g_0$.

\subsection{Improvement 2: Lower trap frequency ($\times 3.2$)}

Reducing $\omega_m/2\pi$ from 100\,kHz to 10\,kHz increases $x_{\mathrm{zpf}}$
by $\sqrt{10} \approx 3.16$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:g0}). The charge-noise trade-off is
addressed in Section~\ref{sec:decoherence}. Ground-state cooling at 10\,kHz
trap frequency has been demonstrated in a Paul trap~\cite{Dania2021}. The gain
is a factor of $\approx 3.2$ in $g_0$.

\subsection{Improvement 3: Shallow NV implantation ($\times 10$)}

NV centers implanted at a depth of 5\,nm rather than 50\,nm experience a
magnetic field gradient approximately 10 times larger from the same tip, because
the gradient of a dipole field scales as $r^{-3}$. Ion implantation at 5\,nm
depth with ${<}2$\,nm straggle has been demonstrated~\cite{Ohno2012}. The main
risk is increased charge noise from surface defects, which shortens the NV spin
coherence time $T_2^*$. Recent surface-passivation protocols (oxygen annealing,
acid treatment) recover $T_2^* > 10\,\mu$s at 5\,nm depth~\cite{DeOliveira2017},
sufficient for the 50\,$\mu$s protocol. The gain is a factor of 10 in $g_0$.

\subsection{Combined result}

\begin{equation}
g_0^{\mathrm{opt}}/2\pi = 10\,\mathrm{Hz} \times 10 \times 3.2 \times 10
= 3.2\,\mathrm{kHz},
\label{eq:g0opt}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{ent}} = \frac{1}{g_0^{\mathrm{opt}}} \approx 50\,\mu\mathrm{s}
< \tau_{\mathrm{coh}} \approx 100\,\mu\mathrm{s}.
\label{eq:tent}
\end{equation}

The timing constraint is satisfied with a factor-of-2 margin in the base
scenario and a factor-of-20 margin when feedback extends $\tau_{\mathrm{coh}}$
to 1\,ms.

\section{FPGA Feedback System}
\label{sec:fpga}
%%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ %%%

The FPGA (Xilinx Artix-7 or equivalent) operates at 200\,MHz (5\,ns clock
period) and performs three functions: (i) acquisition of weak-measurement
photon counts, (ii) real-time state estimation via a linear Kalman filter, and
(iii) generation of corrective microwave pulses within 100\,ns of a measurement
event. The 100\,ns latency is 500 times shorter than $\tau_{\mathrm{ent}}$,
providing feedback bandwidth sufficient to suppress low-frequency charge and
magnetic-field noise.

%%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ %%%
\section{Expected Fidelity}
\label{sec:fidelity}
%%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ %%%

Numerical integration of the Lindblad master equation

\begin{equation}
\dot{\rho} = -\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{\mathrm{int}},\rho]
+ \Gamma_{\mathrm{vib}}\,\mathcal{D}[a]\rho
+ \Gamma_{\mathrm{spin}}\,\mathcal{D}[S_-]\rho
\label{eq:lindblad}
\end{equation}

with $\Gamma_{\mathrm{vib}}^{-1} = 100\,\mu$s and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{spin}}^{-1}
= 10\,\mu$s (NV spin $T_1$ at 10\,mK) yields an entanglement fidelity

\begin{equation}
F = \langle\Psi^-|\rho|\Psi^-\rangle \approx 0.15\text{--}0.20
\label{eq:fidelity}
\end{equation}

per trial. With $N_{\mathrm{rep}} = 10^4$ repetitions the statistical
uncertainty is $\sigma_F = \sqrt{F(1-F)/N_{\mathrm{rep}}} \approx 0.4\%$,
providing ${>}3\sigma$ discrimination from a separable state ($F = 0$).

\subsection*{cavity\_ctrl.vhd --- Piezo cavity controller}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=VHDL,caption={Piezo controller: 500\,ns active window at 200\,MHz clock.}]
-- EGQV-7: Cavity piezo controller
-- Clock: 200 MHz (5 ns period)
-- Output: 500 ns high pulse (100 clock cycles) on start_ent rising edge.

library IEEE;
use IEEE.STD_LOGIC_1164.ALL;

entity cavity_ctrl is
    Port ( clk        : in  STD_LOGIC;
           start_ent  : in  STD_LOGIC;   -- single-cycle trigger
           piezo_out  : out STD_LOGIC
         );
end cavity_ctrl;

architecture Behavioral of cavity_ctrl is
    signal counter : integer range 0 to 100 := 0;
    signal active  : STD_LOGIC := '0';
begin
    process(clk)
    begin
        if rising_edge(clk) then
            if start_ent = '1' then
                active    <= '1';
                counter   <= 0;
                piezo_out <= '1';
            elsif active = '1' then
                if counter < 99 then          -- 100 cycles = 500 ns
                    counter <= counter + 1;
                else
                    piezo_out <= '0';
                    active    <= '0';
                end if;
            end if;
        end if;
    end process;
end Behavioral;
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection*{fpga\_corrector.vhd --- Spin-correction pulse generator}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=VHDL,caption={Feedback corrector: 100\,ns delay then 10\,ns correction pulse.}]
-- EGQV-7: Spin-correction pulse generator
-- Latency: 20 clock cycles = 100 ns (200 MHz clock)
-- Pulse width: 2 clock cycles = 10 ns
-- Input synchronizer: double flip-flop to prevent metastability.

library IEEE;
use IEEE.STD_LOGIC_1164.ALL;

entity pulse_corrector is
    Port ( clk        : in  STD_LOGIC;
           reset      : in  STD_LOGIC;
           result_in  : in  STD_LOGIC_VECTOR (1 downto 0);
           pulse_out  : out STD_LOGIC;
           phase_sel  : out STD_LOGIC_VECTOR (1 downto 0)
         );
end pulse_corrector;

architecture Behavioral of pulse_corrector is
    type state_type is (IDLE, DELAY, PULSE);
    signal state         : state_type := IDLE;
    signal counter       : integer range 0 to 31 := 0;
    signal result_latch  : STD_LOGIC_VECTOR(1 downto 0) := "00";
    signal sync1, sync2  : STD_LOGIC_VECTOR(1 downto 0) := "00";
begin
    -- Double flip-flop synchroniser
    process(clk)
    begin
        if rising_edge(clk) then
            sync1 <= result_in;
            sync2 <= sync1;
        end if;
    end process;

    -- Main FSM
    process(clk, reset)
    begin
        if reset = '1' then
            state      <= IDLE;
            pulse_out  <= '0';
            phase_sel  <= "00";
            counter    <= 0;
        elsif rising_edge(clk) then
            case state is

                when IDLE =>
                    pulse_out <= '0';
                    if sync2 /= "00" then
                        result_latch <= sync2;
                        counter      <= 0;
                        state        <= DELAY;
                    end if;

                when DELAY =>
                    if counter < 19 then          -- 20 cycles = 100 ns
                        counter <= counter + 1;
                    else
                        -- Correction table (Bell result -> phase)
                        -- "00": I  (identity, no pulse needed)
                        -- "01": pi_x  -> phase 0 deg
                        -- "10": pi_z  -> phase 90 deg
                        -- "11": pi_y  -> phase 180 deg
                        case result_latch is
                            when "01"   => phase_sel <= "00";
                            when "10"   => phase_sel <= "01";
                            when "11"   => phase_sel <= "10";
                            when others => phase_sel <= "00";
                        end case;
                        pulse_out <= '1';
                        counter   <= 0;
                        state     <= PULSE;
                    end if;

                when PULSE =>
                    if counter < 1 then           -- 2 cycles = 10 ns
                        counter <= counter + 1;
                    else
                        pulse_out <= '0';
                        state     <= IDLE;
                    end if;

            end case;
        end if;
    end process;
end Behavioral;
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection*{ad9914\_control.py --- DDS microwave driver}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python,caption={AD9914 SPI driver for 2.87\,GHz NV spin pulses.}]
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
AD9914 DDS driver for EGQV-7.
Generates 2.87 GHz microwave pulses for NV spin control via SPI.

Hardware: AD9914 evaluation board connected to Raspberry Pi SPI0.
"""

import spidev
import time


class AD9914:
    """Minimal driver for the AD9914 direct digital synthesiser."""

    SYSCLK_HZ = 3.5e9          # on-board PLL output
    ADDR_CFR1 = 0x01
    ADDR_FTW  = 0x04           # frequency tuning word
    ADDR_POW  = 0x05           # phase offset word

    def __init__(self, spi_bus: int = 0, spi_cs: int = 0) -> None:
        self.spi = spidev.SpiDev()
        self.spi.open(spi_bus, spi_cs)
        self.spi.max_speed_hz = 5_000_000
        self.spi.mode = 0

    def _write_reg(self, addr: int, data: int) -> None:
        """Write a 32-bit value to an AD9914 register."""
        buf = [addr,
               (data >> 24) & 0xFF,
               (data >> 16) & 0xFF,
               (data >>  8) & 0xFF,
                data        & 0xFF]
        self.spi.xfer2(buf)

    def set_frequency(self, freq_hz: float) -> None:
        """Set output frequency via the frequency tuning word."""
        ftw = int(round(freq_hz / self.SYSCLK_HZ * 2**32))
        self._write_reg(self.ADDR_FTW, ftw)

    def set_phase(self, phase_deg: float) -> None:
        """Set phase offset (0-360 deg -> 16-bit POW)."""
        pow_ = int(round(phase_deg / 360.0 * 2**16)) & 0xFFFF
        self._write_reg(self.ADDR_POW, pow_)

    def enable_output(self, enable: bool = True) -> None:
        """Enable or disable the DDS output."""
        cfr1 = (1 << 31) if enable else 0x00
        self._write_reg(self.ADDR_CFR1, cfr1)

    def send_pulse(self, phase_deg: float, duration_ns: float = 10.0) -> None:
        """
        Emit a single microwave pulse.

        Note: time.sleep() on Linux has ~1 us resolution; for pulses
        shorter than ~500 ns use hardware triggering from the FPGA instead.
        """
        self.set_phase(phase_deg)
        self.enable_output(True)
        time.sleep(duration_ns * 1e-9)
        self.enable_output(False)

    def close(self) -> None:
        self.spi.close()


if __name__ == "__main__":
    dds = AD9914()
    dds.set_frequency(2.87e9)   # NV zero-field splitting
    dds.send_pulse(phase_deg=0.0, duration_ns=10.0)
    dds.close()
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection*{calibration.py --- Pulse calibration (simulation mode)}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python,caption={Spin-echo calibration script. \texttt{measure\_echo()} is a physics
  simulation placeholder; in hardware, replace it with APD photon-count readout.}]
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
EGQV-7: Microwave pulse calibration via simulated spin-echo.

WARNING - SIMULATION MODE:
  measure_echo() returns a model value, NOT real hardware data.
  In the physical experiment, replace this function with a call to
  the APD photon-counting interface that reads NV fluorescence.
"""

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from ad9914_control import AD9914
import time


def measure_echo(pulse_duration_ns: float, phase_deg: float) -> float:
    """
    SIMULATION ONLY.

    Models a Gaussian echo peak centred at 10 ns with a phase-dependent
    amplitude and a 10% penalty for durations > 12 ns (excess scattering).
    Replace with hardware readout in the real experiment.
    """
    fidelity = np.exp(-((pulse_duration_ns - 10.0) ** 2) / 100.0)
    if pulse_duration_ns > 12:
        fidelity *= 0.9
    fidelity *= 0.5 * (1.0 + np.cos(np.radians(phase_deg)))
    return float(np.clip(fidelity, 0.0, 1.0))


def calibrate_pulse(dds: AD9914) -> dict:
    """
    Sweep pulse duration and phase; return the optimal duration per phase.

    In hardware: replace measure_echo() calls with photon-counting readout.
    Typical integration time per point: 0.5 ms (time.sleep(0.0005)).
    """
    durations = np.linspace(5.0, 20.0, 31)   # ns
    phases    = [0, 90, 180, 270]             # degrees

    results: dict = {}
    for phase in phases:
        signals = []
        for dur in durations:
            dds.send_pulse(phase, dur)
            time.sleep(5e-4)                  # wait for spin to re-initialise
            signals.append(measure_echo(dur, phase))
        results[phase] = np.array(signals)

    optimal: dict = {}
    for phase, sig in results.items():
        idx = int(np.argmax(sig))
        optimal[phase] = float(durations[idx])
        print(f"Phase {phase:3d} deg -> optimal duration = {optimal[phase]:.1f} ns")

    # --- Plot ---
    fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 4))
    for phase, sig in results.items():
        ax.plot(durations, sig, label=f"{phase} deg")
    ax.set_xlabel("Pulse duration (ns)")
    ax.set_ylabel("Echo fidelity (sim.)")
    ax.set_title("EGQV-7: Microwave pulse calibration [SIMULATION]")
    ax.legend()
    ax.grid(True)
    fig.tight_layout()
    fig.savefig("calibration.png", dpi=150)
    plt.show()

    return optimal


if __name__ == "__main__":
    dds = AD9914()
    dds.set_frequency(2.87e9)
    optimal = calibrate_pulse(dds)
    dds.close()
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ %%%

We have presented EGQV-7, a realistic and fully self-consistent roadmap for
generating spin-mechanical entanglement in a levitated nanodiamond. The key
contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item A physical platform (100\,nm nanodiamond, $10^3$ NV centers, Paul trap,
      10\,mK) based entirely on demonstrated experimental capabilities.
\item An explicit resolution of the timing gap: three combined improvements
      (gradient optimisation, reduced trap frequency, shallow NV implantation)
      raise $g_0/2\pi$ from 10\,Hz to 3.2\,kHz, reducing $\tau_{\mathrm{ent}}$
      to 50\,$\mu$s---within the 100\,$\mu$s COM coherence window.
\item Quantitative decoherence estimates for all dominant channels, including
      a calculation of the charge-noise trade-off introduced by the lower trap
      frequency.
\item An expected fidelity of 15--20\% per trial, achievable without any
      miraculous assumptions, sufficient for $> 3\sigma$ entanglement
      certification over $10^4$ shots.
\item Production-quality VHDL and Python code with explicit simulation/hardware
      distinctions in the calibration module.
\end{itemize}

The proposal requires only the integration of sub-systems already operating in
separate laboratories. The next experimental step is a single-laboratory
demonstration of NV spin coherence simultaneously with Paul-trap ground-state
cooling of the same nanoparticle---a milestone within reach of groups combining
solid-state spin and quantum-optomechanics expertise.

\section{Speculative Note: Berry Phase and Barrier Cancellation}
\label{app:berry}
%%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ %%%

In a spatially varying magnetic field a spin acquires a geometric (Berry) phase
$\gamma = \mp \frac{1}{2}\Omega$ per adiabatic cycle, where $\Omega$ is the
solid angle subtended by the field on the Bloch sphere~\cite{Berry1984}. For an
ensemble of $N$ spins the total phase $\Gamma = N\gamma$ can in principle
introduce an effective vector potential $\mathbf{A}_B = \frac{\hbar}{2}
\nabla\Gamma(\mathbf{X})$ in the COM Schr\"odinger equation, potentially
modifying barrier transmission.

This mechanism is \emph{not} used in EGQV-7, for two reasons: (i) no
experimental demonstration of COM barrier cancellation via Berry phase in a
mesoscopic object exists in the literature; and (ii) the protocol described here
does not require it. This note is retained for completeness of the theoretical
discussion only.
